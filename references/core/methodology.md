# Core Feasibility Methodology

## Purpose
A feasibility study determines whether a defined investment should proceed, under what
configuration, financing structure, scale, timing, and conditions.

It is not a business plan, pitch deck, market report, code review, or valuation memo,
although it may contain elements of each.

## Causal chain
Demand creates the plausible sales envelope.
Capacity and operations constrain what can actually be delivered.
The implementation schedule determines when CAPEX, OPEX, and revenue occur.
Financing determines available liquidity and debt service.
The integrated financial model converts these into profit, balance sheet, and cash.
Investment appraisal evaluates the cash flows.
Scenario, sensitivity, and risk analysis test whether the decision survives uncertainty.

## Core study questions
- Is there evidenced demand at an achievable price?
- Can the project deliver the product/service at required quality and capacity?
- Can it be licensed and operated lawfully?
- What investment and working capital are required?
- What is the timing of cash out and cash in?
- When is additional financing required?
- What is break-even?
- What returns are generated and at what risk?
- Which assumptions can reverse the decision?
- What conditions must be satisfied before capital is committed?

## Evidence classes
FACT: directly supported.
ESTIMATE: calculated from supported data using stated method.
ASSUMPTION: unverified input needed to model the project.
JUDGMENT: interpretation based on evidence.
UNKNOWN: material input unavailable or unresolved.

Every material model input should carry:
ID, value/range, unit, period, geography, evidence class, source/basis, confidence,
validation action, and models/outputs affected.

## Depth by project stage
Idea/pre-feasibility: ranges and tests may dominate.
Full feasibility: driver-based operating and financial model.
Bankable/project-finance: financing and covenant analysis, independent technical/legal
work, project-life cash flows, and lender-style downside cases.
Expansion/acquisition: historical actuals and normalization become primary evidence.
